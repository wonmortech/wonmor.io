## Who pays for the work

Wonmor is a self-funded brand from Uzbekistan. It is not a company yet: there is no legal entity, no board and no investors, and it has taken no outside money. The money that keeps its projects online comes from the founder's own income, and the founder decides how it is spent.

That is a small base, so we are careful with it. This page explains what the money covers, which projects it covers, how a project earns that support, and how we will account for it.

## What Wonmor pays for

| Category | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Servers and infrastructure | The virtual servers the products run on, backups, the edge proxy in front of every site, the self-hosted tunnel and status monitoring. |
| Domains | Registration and renewal of product domains such as dtwin.uz, bitg.uz and wonmor.io. |
| CI minutes | Paid build minutes on GitHub Actions beyond the free allowance: tests, release builds and reproducible installers. |
| Design | Brand work, the brand film, product screenshots and illustrations. |
| Time | The hours the founder spends building, reviewing and maintaining the projects. This time is not billed to anyone. |

This page lists categories only, not amounts. The yearly report described below will say what was covered in each category.

## How projects are chosen

A project gets Wonmor's support when it meets most of these criteria:

- **It is for people we know.** Someone real needs it, and we can ask them whether it works.
- **One person can keep it running.** The design has to be simple enough to operate, back up and restore without a team.
- **It is open where it can be.** Code is public by default; when it is not, the product page says so.
- **It respects privacy and faith.** No tracking for its own sake, and no feature that works against the people using it.
- **It can be measured.** We can tell from numbers, not impressions, whether it is getting better.

Support grows with the project. Each product page shows a Wonmor Release Level (WRL), and each level asks for more care:

```steps
WRL-1 · Prototype :: A working demo. Support covers time and a small server, nothing more.
WRL-2 · MVP :: Continuous integration and backups are in place before real people rely on it.
WRL-3 · Production :: Status monitoring, a tested rollback and a security review.
WRL-4 · Critical :: Reproducible builds, signed provenance and an OpenSSF Best Practices badge.
```

When a project stops meeting the criteria, we say so on its page and archive it openly instead of leaving it half-maintained.

## The yearly report (planned)

Planned: a yearly report on what Wonmor supported. The first one will cover 2026 and will be published after the year ends. It will list each category above, the projects each one served, and what changed during the year. Whether it includes exact amounts is still being decided. Either way, it will contain only real spending: nothing estimated and nothing rounded up to look bigger.

## Wonmor Open Source Fund (planned)

Planned: the **Wonmor Open Source Fund**, a way to support independent open-source builders in Uzbekistan beyond the founder's own projects. It depends on Wonmor first becoming a legal entity, which is also planned. When the fund opens, its criteria, application template and reports will be public. Until then, no applications are accepted and no money has been promised to anyone.

## What this page does not mean

Wonmor is not a grant-maker, an accelerator or an investor. It does not buy equity, does not take a share of revenue and has no sponsorship deals with outside companies. If any of that changes, this page will change first.
